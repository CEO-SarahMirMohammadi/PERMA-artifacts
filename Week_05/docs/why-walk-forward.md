A single train/test split can be misleading because one lucky or unlucky period can dominate the result, while a rolling walk-forward evaluation repeatedly refits the model on a fixed training window and tests on the next block of observations, making the assessment more realistic and less prone to look-ahead leakage. For each fold, we record the mean absolute error (MAE), then summarize the set of window-level errors with their mean and standard deviation so we can see whether the model is stable across different market regimes instead of relying on one answer. This approach still does not guarantee profitability, because forecast error alone does not account for transaction costs, slippage, liquidity, or decision rules; it simply measures the quality of the prediction process.
