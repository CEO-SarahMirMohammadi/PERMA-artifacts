# Why Feature Scaling Matters in Machine Learning

Feature scaling transforms numerical features to comparable scales so that variables with large numerical values do not dominate algorithms simply because of their units. For example, trading volume may be measured in thousands while returns and volatility are small decimal values. Some algorithms, including gradient-based models and distance-based methods, can behave poorly when feature scales differ significantly. Standardization (subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation) and normalization are common approaches, but the appropriate method depends on the model and data. Scaling parameters should be fitted on training data only and then applied to validation and test data to prevent data leakage. Tree-based models generally require less feature scaling than distance-based or gradient-based models.
